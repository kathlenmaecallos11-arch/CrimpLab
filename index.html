What is crimping?

Crimping presses a connector onto the end of a cable so it holds firmly and makes electrical contact.

UTP Ethernet cable (Cat5e/Cat6)

Unshielded twisted pair cable contains eight color-coded wires, arranged as four twisted pairs, used to connect network devices. The twists reduce interference. Cat5e and Cat6 are the common choices and both carry gigabit Ethernet up to 100 meters.

RJ45 / 8P8C connector

A plastic plug with eight gold contacts. Each contact presses into one wire when crimped. It comes in two styles: closed-end and pass-through.

Why crimp your own?

You get the exact length you need, lower cost, and the skill to repair a damaged end.

Wiring standards

T568A and T568B are both accepted wiring standards. Pick a standard to see the color order, then tap a wire to highlight its pin.

    Tip: View the connector with the gold contacts facing up and the plug's clip facing away/down as instructed by your connector manufacturer. Pin numbering and viewing orientation can vary in diagrams, so follow the pin-1 orientation shown in your reference.

    T568A vs T568B, pin by pin

    Only pins 1, 2, 3 and 6 differ. The highlighted rows are where the two standards swap the green and orange pairs.

    Cable types

    The cable type is decided by the wiring standard you use on each end.

    Straight-through

    A–A or B–B

    T568A on both ends, or T568B on both ends. Commonly used to connect different device types, such as a PC to a switch.

    Crossover

    A–B

    T568A on one end and T568B on the other. Traditionally used to directly connect similar devices. Many modern devices support Auto-MDI/MDI-X and may not require a crossover cable.
